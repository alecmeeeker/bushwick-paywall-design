Why a fixed body and not Portal's overflow:hidden: - overflow:hidden on <body> did not stop touch scrolling on iOS. WebKit bug 153852, " with overflow:hidden CSS is scrollable on iOS", is RESOLVED FIXED. Its patch landed 2019-05-07. A commenter on 2019-09-21 reported that it landed in iOS 13 "but there are cases where it doesn't work". WebKit itself names no release. - A fixed body cannot scroll in any engine, whatever that engine does with overflow on <body>. - Cost: on desktop, the site's sticky header moves off-screen behind the blurred scrim while the sheet is open.

Why not <dialog>.showModal(): - showModal() makes everything outside the dialog inert, including elements added later. - Stripe "automatically displays the authentication UI in a pop-up modal" for 3-D Secure (docs.stripe.com, "Authenticate with 3D Secure"). Where Stripe attaches that modal is not documented (UNVERIFIED). If it lands outside a modal <dialog>, the reader could not complete 3-D Secure. - BDSheet.inert() marks only the <body> children that exist at open time, so a later Stripe overlay stays usable. inert is "Baseline, widely available … since April 2023" (MDN). - z-index 100000 sits above every fixed layer the article prints: progress bar 9999 (single.php:263), newsletter popup 10000 (:1278), Support button 99990 (bd-support-cta.php:21).

Layout: - Phones (< 768 px): the sheet is 100% of the viewport (100dvh) and opaque (#0a0a0a), with the 3 px yellow rule, a top bar (← Back, the site's SVG wordmark, X) and one scroll box. - Safe-area padding uses env(safe-area-inset-*). These values are 0 today, because single.php:177 has no viewport-fit=cover, so content stays inside the safe area. The build does not add viewport-fit=cover. - On the plans step, Continue is pinned to the bottom of the screen (position: sticky), so it is visible on any phone height. Measured: Continue at y 604–652 on 664. - From 768 px (single.php:320): a 48rem panel, centred, with an 8px radius, over the page dimmed to rgba(10,10,10,.72) and blurred 6 px. - The plans step fits in 1440×900 without scrolling inside the panel. Measured panel: 824–827 px tall. - Clicking the dimmed page does nothing. Decision: a stray click must not discard a typed card number. The exits are X, Escape and Back.

7. Rationale on the three axes (code quality · architecture by external standards · journalism)

  • G-1 Site design system. Code: one token set instead of the DiMieri page's. Architecture: a component takes its look from the design system of the page it serves (the DEBT report's three-tier token model). Journalism: the reader is asked to pay by the paper they are reading.
  • G-2 One offer per page. Code: the layout pass stops placing a second offer. Architecture: one call to action per view is the standard conversion pattern; two prices for "membership" on one screen is a contradiction. Journalism: a reader is never shown "$15/mo" and "$6.99/month" for the same word at the same moment. (The $15 tote tiers remain on /subscribe/, the nav and the footer; grandfathered supporters are entitled at the gate, GATE-FUNNEL-SPEC S2a.)
  • G-3 No sticky bar. Owner decision; it also removes a second email field on screen while S1 asks for one.
  • G-4 Legacy sheet off. Code: removes !important overrides the gate cannot beat. Architecture: a template that owns its CSS does not load a sheet written for another theme. Journalism: phone readers (the larger share is UNVERIFIED) see the same paper as desktop readers.
  • G-5 Lead-only layout. Code: the planner stops counting UI markup as story paragraphs (measured defect). Architecture: layout passes operate on content, components are appended. Journalism: no ad or band between the last public paragraph and the gate.
  • G-6 Component isolation. DEBT D8 (not-prose, the Tailwind Typography convention); cascade layers rejected there with the MDN reason. Journalism: the gate renders as designed on every article.
  • G-7 S1 order. Code: DOM order = visual order = tab order. Architecture: options before the submit action is the standard form order. Journalism: the email step is the newsletter signup (owner); the newsletter is offered as part of the step, not after it.
  • G-8 No eyebrow. Hell Gate weight (21 words at the gate). Fewer words before the field.
  • G-9/G-10 Copy and alert. The screen says what is on it; an error appears where the reader is looking.
  • G-11 Confirmation after payment. A payment needs an on-screen receipt; a sign-in does not.
  • G-12 Black wallet button. Owner rule kept; the stand-in is labelled as such.
  • G-13 Appearance. Only documented variables; Stripe's iframe gets the site's UI font.
  • G-14 Emails. The register the owner directed for the newsletter; one inbox look for everything Bushwick Daily sends. Fluid container: the register's fixed width="600" table makes Chromium lay a 390px phone out at 600px and scale it to 65% (measured: scrollWidth 600 for welcome-email-refined.html and onboarding-e1-tote.html on the iPhone 13 profile); the proposed width="100%" + max-width:600px lays out at 390.   • G-15/G-16/G-17 Frame and logo. One wordmark source; the account, terms and DiMieri pages carry the paper's mark at load.
